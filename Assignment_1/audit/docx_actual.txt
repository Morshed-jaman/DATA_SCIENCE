3.3 Normality limitations and assumptions
UI_A uses all 5,013 observations. SciPy issued the following warning; no subsampling was performed:
UserWarning: scipy.stats.shapiro: For N > 5000, computed p-value may not be accurate. Current N is 5013.
For more than 5,000 observations, SciPy documents that W remains accurate but the p-value may not. Treat its precise magnitude cautiously. UI_B has 4,987 observations and did not trigger this warning. Large samples make normality tests sensitive, but the observed curvature and skewness also show substantial non-normality [1].
The distribution of individual durations is not the sampling distribution of a mean. Under independence and finite population variances, large samples support approximate mean inference even with non-normal raw data. Independence is assumed from the assignment scenario; unique IDs do not establish it. Large samples do not repair dependence or biased sampling. Welch addresses unequal variances, not raw-data normality.
4. Variance assessment and Welch test
Median-centered Levene (Brown-Forsythe) gives statistic 65.919294, p = 5.25152025e-16. It is robust to departures from normality, especially skewness [2]. The assignment rule selects Welch because p < 0.05. A non-significant result would not prove equal variances; preliminary variance-based selection is data-dependent and can affect overall error properties.
Let mA, mB be sample means; sA, sB sample SDs; and nA, nB sample sizes. Define vA = sA²/nA and vB = sB²/nB.
SE = √(vB + vA);   t = (mB − mA)/SE
df = (vB + vA)² / [vB²/(nB − 1) + vA²/(nA − 1)]
p = 2 × t.sf(|t|, df)
CI₉₅ = (mB − mA) ± t.ppf(0.975, df) × SE
vB = 83.64436110²/4987 = 1.4029234298; vA = 71.56361982²/5013 = 1.0216141396.
SE = √(1.4029234298 + 1.0216141396) = 1.55709267 seconds. t = (126.25848606 − 99.66307401)/1.55709267 = 17.08017297.
df = (1.40292343 + 1.02161414)² / [1.40292343²/4986 + 1.02161414²/5012] = 9748.82914022.

5. Required normal-model probability scenarios
XA ~ Normal(mA, sA²);   XB ~ Normal(mB, sB²)
mA = 99.66307401, sA = 71.56361982; mB = 126.25848606, sB = 83.64436110 seconds. Use individual-session SDs, not standard errors. All displayed substitutions are rounded only for readability.
These are the normal-model approximations required by the assignment. Both raw distributions are right-skewed and non-normal; large samples do not make individual durations normal. The requested model is not truncated, even though it allows negative theoretical durations. Empirical comparisons are reported separately.
5.1 Worked probability answers
(a) P(XA > mB)
A UI_A session exceeds the UI_B sample mean.
zlower = (126.25848606 − 99.66307401) / 71.56361982 = 0.37163313.
1 − Φ(z) = norm.sf(0.37163313) = 0.35508301 = 35.5083%.
Under this model, about 35.51% of individual UI_A sessions satisfy the event.
UI_B’s full-precision sample mean is a fixed threshold here. This is not P(XA > XB).
(b) P(XA < 80)
A UI_A session lasts less than 80 seconds.
zupper = (80.00000000 − 99.66307401) / 71.56361982 = -0.27476355.
Φ(z) = norm.cdf(-0.27476355) = 0.39174895 = 39.1749%.
Under this model, about 39.17% of individual UI_A sessions satisfy the event.
(c) P(XB > 150)
A UI_B session lasts more than 150 seconds.
zlower = (150.00000000 − 126.25848606) / 83.64436110 = 0.28383879.
1 − Φ(z) = norm.sf(0.28383879) = 0.38826696 = 38.8267%.
Under this model, about 38.83% of individual UI_B sessions satisfy the event.
(d) P(80 < XA < 120)
A UI_A session lasts strictly between 80 and 120 seconds.
zlower = (80.00000000 − 99.66307401) / 71.56361982 = -0.27476355.
zupper = (120.00000000 − 99.66307401) / 71.56361982 = 0.28417967.
Φ(zupper) − Φ(zlower) = Φ(0.28417967) − Φ(-0.27476355) = 0.22011470 = 22.0115%.
Under this model, about 22.01% of individual UI_A sessions satisfy the event.

6. Business impact and conclusion
Absolute difference = mB − mA
= 126.25848606 − 99.66307401 = 26.59541206 seconds.
Relative increase (%) = 100 × (mB − mA)/mA
= 100 × (126.25848606 − 99.66307401) / 99.66307401 = 26.685322%.
This is a relative percentage increase in mean session duration, not a change in percentage points. The verified Welch result is statistically significant (p = 1.79827227e-64); its 95% interval for UI_B minus UI_A is [23.543188, 29.647637] seconds.
If longer engagement is the business objective, UI_B is a candidate for adoption, conditional on practical value, costs, and checks of relevant outcomes. Longer sessions alone do not prove better usability, satisfaction, conversion, or revenue; they may also reflect friction. The evidence establishes a difference in mean duration under the stated assumptions, not a universal improvement in user experience.
